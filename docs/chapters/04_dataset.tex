\section{Mapping audio signals to output classes}
\label{sec:dataset_overview}

The mapping of audio signals to the three output classes defined in \Cref{sec:output_classes} is grounded in the audio-signal categorization of Al-Shoshan~\cite[p.~97]{alshoshan2006review}, which partitions audio into nine categories (paraphrased from the original):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Speech signal composed of a single talker within a specific time period.
    \item Pure music signal without any speech component.
    \item Mixture of single-talker speech and background music.
    \item Songs, i.e., a mixture of music and a singer's voice.
    \item Singing without musical accompaniment.
    \item Abnormal music, including unconventional cadences, human whistles, or non-musical sounds embedded as part of a musical structure.
    \item Speech signal composed of two or more speakers talking simultaneously.
    \item Non-speech and non-music signals, such as car, motor, or fan sounds.
    \item Complex sound mixtures involving multiple speakers, singers, and musical sources.
\end{enumerate}

The nine categories are projected onto the three output classes as follows.
Categories 1, 3, and 7 (single-talker speech, speech with background music, multi-talker speech) map to \emph{Speech}.
Categories 2, 4, 5, 6, and 9 (pure music, songs, unaccompanied singing, atypical music, complex multi-source mixtures) map to \emph{Music}.
Category 8 and pure silence map to \emph{Background}.

\subsection{Frame-level labeling}
\label{sec:dataset_labeling}

Each clip carries a list of timestamped label intervals rather than a single clip-level tag.
A label entry is a triple (\emph{Class}, start, end), where the start/end timestamps are millisecond offsets into the clip with the interval treated as half-open.
A clip's full manifest is the concatenation of these intervals, covering the clip with no gaps.

This per-frame formulation is what makes the dataset usable for streaming evaluation (\Cref{sec:exp_evaluation}).
A single clip can contain a transition between speech and music, or a stretch of background audio bridging two speech segments, and every frame in the clip has its own ground-truth label.
The same format is used uniformly across the dataset.
Clean speech receives \emph{Speech} intervals spanning the clip duration interleaved with \emph{Background} intervals during the breaks in speech.
The synthetic SoM augmentation interleaves \emph{Speech} and \emph{Music} intervals.
Critical-subset clips carry as many intervals as the manual annotation requires.

\subsection{Split policy}
\label{sec:dataset_split}

The full subset uses an $84\,\% / 8\,\% / 8\,\%$ split for train, validation, and test, applied per source per subclass.
This guarantees that every subclass appears in every partition even when a source contributes very few clips.

\section{Dataset construction methodology}
\label{sec:dataset_composition}

The build pipeline proceeds in three stages, summarized in \Cref{fig:dataset_pipeline}.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/dataset/dataset_pipeline.pdf}
	\caption{Dataset construction pipeline.}
\label{fig:dataset_pipeline}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1} produces the real subclasses from external sources via class-specific labelers.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2} generates four synthetic speech subclasses by mixing real subclasses at build time.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3} interleaves the recordings into the three splits per source (\Cref{sec:dataset_split}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Sources and automatic labeling}

The complete enumeration of external sources is given in \Cref{apx:dataset_sources}.
The pipeline groups them by output class and applies a class-specific labeler to each clip.

\paragraph{Speech.}
\emph{Speech} material is drawn entirely from LibriSpeech ASR~\cite{panayotov2015librispeech}.
The clean training split supplies the clean speech subclass and the other training split supplies dirty speech.
Each clip is processed by Silero VAD~\cite{silero2024vad} to identify speech-active regions.
Clips are split at silence boundaries and only the speech-active segments are retained.

\paragraph{Music.}
\emph{Music} material combines the Free Music Archive (FMA)~\cite{defferrard2017fma} with the Bel Canto a cappella dataset from CCMusic~\cite{zhou2025ccmusic}.
FMA contributes six genres in equal proportion: instrumental, electronic, pop, rock, hip-hop, and folk.
Bel Canto contributes the seventh subclass, a cappella music, which is the music subclass closest to speech in feature space.
Its inclusion forces the model to learn a clean speech-vs-vocal-music boundary at training time and exposes any remaining errors at evaluation.
A custom music labeler combines a pyannote-audio voice-activity segmentation model~\cite{bredin2023pyannote,plaquet2023diarization} with a root-mean-square (RMS)-based loudness threshold.
The threshold is computed over $20$\,ms windows, with the per-clip noise floor set at the $20$th percentile of RMS values scaled by $0.35$.
A frame is labeled music if the segmentation model identifies it as speech-active (instrumental music is similar enough to speech to register on this voice-activity model) or if its RMS exceeds the noise floor.

\paragraph{Background.}
Environmental noise is drawn from the DEMAND dataset~\cite{thiemann2013demand}, recordings spanning office, traffic, kitchen, and outdoor environments.
Labeling is trivial: every clip is tagged \emph{Background} as a whole.

\subsection{Synthetic augmentations}
\label{sec:dataset_augmentations}

Four synthetic subclasses fill in deployment-relevant conditions that no real-audio source covers cleanly.
Each is generated deterministically at build time from the speech, music, and noise sources above, and carries the \emph{Speech} class label.
\Cref{tab:dataset_synthetic_augs} summarizes the four constructions.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.70\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Subclass} & \textbf{Construction} \\
\midrule
multi-speaker & Two LibriSpeech speakers overlaid at $-5$ to $+5$\,dB relative gain. \\
SoM           & LibriSpeech with FMA music attenuated by $10$\,dB. \\
MSoM          & Multi-speaker overlay placed over attenuated FMA music. \\
noisy         & LibriSpeech with DEMAND noise at $5$ to $20$\,dB SNR. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
	\caption{Synthetic speech subclasses generated at build time.}
\label{tab:dataset_synthetic_augs}
\end{table}

Frame labels follow an overlay rule: the base clip's \emph{Speech} label applies throughout, and on its \emph{Background} intervals the secondary clip's labels take over.
For SoM this gives \emph{Speech} where the LibriSpeech speaker is active and \emph{Music} during the silences.
The same rule produces multi-speaker, MSoM, and noisy speech by analogy.

\section{Critical subset}
\label{sec:dataset_critical_subset}

The critical subset is a small collection of $113$ clips ($\sim 75$\,min total) built late in the work to sanity-check the models under known stress conditions.
Clips are hand-selected by stress condition and labeled manually.

\subsection{Methodology}

The critical subset is built from $25$ recordings hand-picked from publicly accessible YouTube material:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $8$ speech sources: six clean, one multi-speaker, one whispered.
    \item $10$ music sources: seven in-distribution genres plus jumpup, speedcore, and beatbox as out-of-distribution stressors.
    \item $7$ background-noise sources covering airport, birdsong, cafeteria, cooler fan, fireplace, rain, and street, each acting as a direct stressor clip and as mixing material for the noisy-speech generator.
\end{itemize}
Frame-level labels were produced manually rather than through automatic labelers, for ground-truth precision.

Two build-time routines expand the source pool into the $113$-clip, $\sim 75$\,min subset.
A noisy-speech generator forms the Cartesian product of the six clean-speech sources, the seven noise sources, and two SNR levels ($0$\,dB and $-3$\,dB), producing $84$ noisy-speech clips.

A speech-over-music (SoM) mixer overlays clean speech onto music at fixed gain offsets, producing $2$ SoM and $2$ MSoM clips from manually labeled sources.

Frame labels on the synthetic clips follow the same overlay rule as the full subset's augmentations (\Cref{sec:dataset_augmentations}).

\subsection{Coverage}

The critical subset mixes two groups of clips: stressors and in-distribution references.

Stressors are the focus of the subset, each one isolating a condition expected to challenge the classifiers.
\Cref{tab:dataset_crit_subclasses} summarizes the conditions exposed.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lp{0.62\textwidth}}

\textbf{Stressor} & \textbf{Stress condition} \\
\midrule

In-distribution references repeat most standard subclasses for sanity comparison against a model's behavior on familiar material.
Dirty speech is left out since the noisy-speech augmentations already cover degraded speech.
